# Feelings Are Not Facts

You can feel something deeply, and it still might not be true. Feelings aren't facts — they're information. They tell you something is happening, but they don't get the final word on what's real.

## The Opposite of Anxiety Isn't Calm

It's tolerance.

Not the absence of discomfort, but the ability to sit with uncertainty without letting it run the show. Calm is a nice byproduct — but tolerance is the actual skill.

## Your Patterns Made Sense Once

Most of the habits and reactions you're trying to unlearn weren't random. They were built for a version of your life that no longer exists — a younger you, a harder situation, a different set of circumstances.

Understanding that doesn't excuse the pattern. But it does change how you see yourself while you're working to change it.

## Avoidance Always Works — At First

That's exactly why it's so hard to stop.

Every time you avoid something, your brain files it away as confirmation: *that thing was dangerous.* The relief is real, but it's short-term, and it quietly reinforces the fear it was supposed to relieve.

## You Can't Pour From an Empty Cup

And you can't think your way to a full one.

Rest isn't a reward you earn after everything else is done. It's a requirement — the same way sleep or food is a requirement. Skipping it doesn't make you more productive; it just runs you on empty.

## Insight Isn't Enough

Understanding *why* you do something doesn't automatically change what you do. Repetition does.

You don't think your way into a new pattern — you practice your way there. Insight opens the door. Repetition is what walks you through it.

## You Are Not Your Worst Moment

You are not your thoughts, your history, or your hardest moments.

You are what you repeatedly choose to do next.

---

*None of this is about fixing yourself overnight. It's about noticing the pattern, choosing differently — even slightly — and doing it again tomorrow.*
